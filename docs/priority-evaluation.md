# Priority Model Evaluation

Generated by `PriorityCalibrationTest`. Re-run with:

```bash
cd backend-spring && ./mvnw test -Dtest=PriorityCalibrationTest
```

This is the evidence ISSUE-08 asked for before the weights are
declared final. It is a SYNTHETIC corpus: it can show the model is
broken, and it cannot show the model is right. Only labelled real
incidents (Phase 8) can do that.

## Corpus

- 2000 incidents across 243 wards and 8 categories
- members: min 1, median 6, p95 17, max 79
- 757 incidents (38%) had at least one report in the last 24 h
- 977 incidents (49%) are 30 days or older (the age saturation point)
- 472 incidents (24%) are spread over 1 km or more
- 5% of incidents deliberately carry NO coordinates

## Term influence (ablation)

Each term is neutralised in turn and the corpus re-scored. "Mean |delta|"
is how far scores move; "top-50 churn" is how many of the fifty
highest-priority incidents change when the term is removed — the
measure that matters, since that is the part of the queue anyone
actually looks at.

| Term | Weight | Mean \|delta\| | Max \|delta\| | Top-50 churn |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| volume | 40% | 1.856 | 6.50 | 47 / 50 |
| growth | 30% | 0.334 | 3.00 | 23 / 50 |
| age | 20% | 1.474 | 2.00 | 18 / 50 |
| spread | 10% | 0.746 | 4.00 | 19 / 50 |

## Term saturation

A term pinned at its ceiling (or floor) for most of the corpus is not
ranking anything — it is adding a constant. This is what the weights
cannot tell you and is the single most useful number here for Phase 8.

| Term | Saturated (= 1.0) | Zero | Discriminating |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| volume (cap 25) | 2.7% | 8.7% | 88.7% |
| growth (cap 5/24h) | 2.3% | 62.2% | 35.6% |
| age (cap 30 d) | 48.9% | 2.6% | 48.6% |
| spread (cap 1 km) | 23.6% | 5.3% | 71.2% |

**The finding this file carried into Phase 8.** With age saturating at
14 days, age was pinned at 1.0 for 77% of this corpus — a near-constant
+2.0 on most scores, which changed the numbers without changing the
ordering much. **Since 2026-10-22 age saturates at 30 days**, after a
blind proxy ranking preferred 30 on both halves in every run
(docs/priority-calibration-proxy.md); it is now pinned for 49%, and
keeps discriminating for as long as incidents actually stay open.

Usable range now: p25 3.6 to p95 6.4, with 66% of incidents in the
most common band. A human ranking should still confirm the change; the
next candidate is a category severity term, which the proxy's
rationales pointed to and the formula does not have.

## DECISION

**Ship the model: weights 40/30/20/10, bands 3.0 / 5.5 / 7.5, age
saturating at 30 days.** Approved 2026-09-17 with age at 14 days;
amended 2026-10-22 to 30 days on a proxy calibration.

## Phase 8 — the officer session (tooling ready 2026-10-21)

This is the real-world check the DECISION above waits for. Everything except
the officer's time is built.

**1. Export, both files in the same minute** (signed in as an officer; the age
term moves daily, so a key fetched a day later no longer matches the sheet):

- `/api/core/dashboard/ranking-sheet` → `ranking-sheet-<date>.csv`, for the
  officer. It lists every active incident with the facts the formula reads
  (reports, reports in the last 24 h and 7 days, days open, spread) and
  nothing derived from them: no score, no band, no stored reasons (those are
  ordered by contribution, which would give the ranking away). Rows are in a
  fixed scrambled order. `?wardId=` narrows it to one officer's wards.
- `/api/core/dashboard/ranking-sheet?withScores=true` → `ranking-key-<date>.csv`,
  for the analyst. The same rows plus the formula's score and band. **Do not
  show it to the officer.**

**2. The session.** The officer fills `officer_rank`: 1 = deal with first,
no ties, at least 30 incidents (blank rows are skipped). Two officers, ranking
separately, is better: their agreement with each other is the ceiling the
formula can be held to.

**3. Score it:**

```bash
cd backend-node
npm run eval:priority -- ranking-key.csv ranked-officer-a.csv [ranked-officer-b.csv]
```

It first checks that its copy of the formula (`src/eval/priorityMetrics.ts`)
reproduces every score in the key. Then it reports:

- Kendall τ-b, formula against officer;
- precision@10;
- each term alone against the officer;
- the variants named above: age saturation 30 d, age saturation 45 d, and age
  weight 10%;
- every variant on two fixed halves of the incidents;
- officer against officer, when two sheets are given.

Paste its markdown here.

**4. Decide (roadmap 8.4).** Adopt a variant only if it beats the shipped
formula on **both** halves. A gain on one half alone is the variant fitting
those incidents. Change `PriorityService` and the `PRODUCTION` constants in
`priorityMetrics.ts` together. `npm test` fails if they disagree on the
probes.

## What this does and does not establish

**Established.** The model discriminates: all four bands are reachable
and critical is 2.0% of the corpus rather than most of it. Ordering is
operationally sensible on every probe above — a growing incident
outranks a static one of equal size, age alone cannot manufacture
urgency, spread nudges rather than dominates, and more corroboration
never lowers a score. No ward or category is structurally advantaged,
because the formula never reads either.

**Not established.** Whether these are the RIGHT weights. A synthetic
corpus inherits the generator's assumptions about how civic complaints
actually arrive, and the generator was written by the same hand as the
formula. Phase 8 must label real incidents — an officer ranking a real
morning's queue — before any weight or threshold is called final.

**Do not** re-tune weights from this file alone. Its purpose is to fail
loudly if a change breaks something obvious, which is exactly what a
calibration pass needs underneath it.
